Privacy Policy

The short version: Dawn Player is a player, not a service. There is nothing to sign up for, and no server of ours for your data to sit on.

Last updated: 24 August 2026  ·  Applies to: Dawn Player for Android, Android TV, iOS and Windows

Dawn Player collects no personal data. No user accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, no tracking of any kind. The developer receives nothing about you or your viewing, because there is no server of ours in the data path.

Your playlists, provider credentials, watch history and settings are stored on your own device. Video streams travel directly from your provider to your device.

Who this policy is from

Dawn Player is an independent, personally developed application. In this policy, “the app” means Dawn Player and “we” means its developer. Contact details are at the bottom of this page.

What Dawn Player is, and what that means for your data

Dawn Player does not provide, host, resell or aggregate any television channels, films or series. It is an empty player until you point it at an Xtream Codes or M3U playlist you already hold from a provider of your own choosing.

That shapes the whole privacy picture, because it determines who sees what. Your provider sees your viewing, because your device requests streams directly from your provider's servers — exactly as it would with any other player. We are not in the middle of that connection and could not observe it if we wanted to.

Data stored on your device

All of the following stays on the device and is never transmitted to us:

  • Provider credentials. Your Xtream Codes username and password, or your M3U playlist URL, held in the operating system's secure credential store — the iOS Keychain, or Android's Keystore-backed encrypted storage. They are never written to ordinary app storage or to logs.
  • Your catalogue. Channels, films and series read from your playlist, plus any EPG (programme guide) data, cached in a local database so the app opens instantly and keeps working while offline.
  • Watch history and resume positions — what you watched and how far in you got.
  • Preferences — favourites, hidden and renamed channels and groups, preferred audio and subtitle languages, and player settings.
  • Cached artwork — poster and backdrop images, stored so they are not re-fetched on every scroll.

Uninstalling the app deletes all of it. You can also clear parts of it from inside the app: removing an account removes its credentials and its catalogue with it.

Network connections the app makes

Dawn Player makes no network request you have not caused. There is no background reporting, no heartbeat, no telemetry. Every destination below is either a server you configured or one you asked the app to reach; two of the five are off unless you turn them on. In full:

Your IPTV provider When you refresh your catalogue or play something Your credentials, to authenticate as the provider requires; the stream you asked for; and your device's IP address. Governed by your provider's privacy policy, not this one.
Your EPG source When the programme guide refreshes A request for the XMLTV guide file, to whatever URL your playlist or your settings specify — usually the same provider as above.

A sync server you choose Only if you turn sync on and supply a server address and token. Off by default. Your watch history and preferences, so they follow you between your own devices. This goes to your server at your address, not to us. Leave sync off, as it ships, and nothing ever leaves the device this way.

Notably absent, deliberately: no advertising network, no analytics or attribution SDK, no crash or performance reporting, no social login, and no server operated by us. The app's typefaces are bundled inside it rather than fetched from a font CDN, so even rendering its own interface involves no third party.

Data we collect

None. We operate no servers that receive data from the app, so there is nothing for us to collect, store, share, sell or lose. Because we hold no data about you, a request to access, correct, export or delete “your data” has no subject — but the next section covers how to erase everything the app holds locally.

Deleting your data

  • Everything at once: uninstall Dawn Player. The operating system removes all local storage, including the credential store and the cached catalogue.
  • One provider: Settings → Accounts → remove the account. Its credentials, catalogue and cached artwork go with it.
  • Sync data: the sync server is one you supply, so deleting data there is done on that server. We have no access to it.

Children

Dawn Player is not directed at children and is not designed for use by them. It collects no data from anyone, children included. Because the app plays whatever your provider's playlist contains, and we neither select nor filter that content, it is intended for adults who are responsible for the source they connect.

Security

Credentials are held in the platform's own secure credential store rather than in application storage. Where your provider offers HTTPS, connections use it. Many IPTV providers still serve over plain HTTP; the app permits that, because otherwise it could not play those streams at all. It does mean the connection between your device and your provider may be unencrypted. That is a property of your provider rather than of the app, and it is worth asking them about.

Third-party content and attribution

Dawn Player ships no channels, films or series, and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to any content provider. Some artwork and metadata come from TMDB, only when you supply your own API key: this product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB. The bundled list of award-winning titles derives from Wikidata (CC0) and Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0), and is used only to recognise titles that already exist in your own playlist.

Changes to this policy

If the app's behaviour changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top is updated. Because the app has no accounts, we have no way to notify you directly; material changes are noted in the app's release notes.
