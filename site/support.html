Support

Most problems with an IPTV player are really problems with the line behind it. Here is how to tell which one you have.

Last updated: 24 August 2026

Dawn Player does not sell or supply television. It ships with no channels, no films and no provider. To use it you need your own Xtream Codes login or M3U playlist URL from a service you already subscribe to. We cannot provide one, recommend one, or help with an account you hold with a provider.

Getting in touch

One person answers this address, usually within a few days:

support@dawnplayer.com

To get a useful answer first time, please include: your device and OS version, the app version and build (Settings → About & credits), whether your source is Xtream Codes or M3U, and what exactly you saw — the wording of any error, and whether it affects one channel or all of them. Please don't send your provider password.

Setting up

How do I add my playlist?

Settings → Accounts → add an account, then pick the kind you have:

  • Xtream Codes — the server address, your username and your password. Your provider gives you all three. Enter the server exactly as they wrote it, including http:// or https:// and the port number if there is one.
  • M3U — the full playlist URL, or a playlist file on the device.

The first load reads your whole catalogue and can take a minute or two on a large line. After that it opens from the local copy and refreshes in the background.

“Could not connect” when I add an account

Almost always one of four things:

  • A typo in the server address, or a missing port number.
  • http:// where the provider wants https://, or the reverse.
  • Credentials that have expired, or a subscription that has lapsed — try them in your provider's own web portal to check.
  • Your provider limits how many devices or connections can be active at once, and another device is holding the slot.
Where do I get a playlist or an Xtream login?

From a provider of your own choosing — we are not one and cannot point you at one. Dawn Player is a player, in the same sense that a web browser is not a website.

Playback

A channel buffers, stutters or won't start

First, work out whether it is the app or the line. If one channel misbehaves while the rest are fine, the problem is that stream at your provider, and nothing in the app will fix it. If everything stutters, suspect the connection between you and them.

Worth trying, in order: a wired connection or 5 GHz Wi-Fi instead of 2.4 GHz; pausing other large downloads; and asking your provider whether they offer a nearer server or a lower bitrate of the same channel. Live IPTV has no buffer to fall back on the way an on-demand service does, so a brief network dip is immediately visible.

There's sound but no picture, or the wrong audio track

Open the track menu in the player and choose explicitly — Dawn Player remembers the audio and subtitle language you pick and applies it to everything you open afterwards, so you only need to do this once per language.

A stream with no picture at all usually carries a video codec the device cannot decode. Playback uses libmpv, which handles far more formats than the system player, but a hardware decoder it cannot fall back from will still fail. Report it with the channel name and we can look at the stream's format.

Resume forgot where I was

Resume positions are recorded per title, on the device. They are not shared between devices unless you have turned on sync and pointed it at a server of your own. If a title's identity changed at your provider — a renamed entry, or a different stream id after a catalogue rebuild — the app can no longer match it to the old position.

Guide, catalogue and artwork

The programme guide is empty or wrong

The guide comes from your provider's XMLTV data, not from us. If it is empty, they may not publish one, or the URL in your playlist may be stale. If times are consistently offset, their guide is published in a different timezone than it claims; if titles are missing for some channels only, their guide simply does not cover those channels.

Films and series have no posters

Many playlists carry no artwork at all. Dawn Player can fill the gaps from TMDB, but only with an API key you supply: Settings → add your own TMDB key. Keys are free for personal use from themoviedb.org. Without one the app still works and falls back to clean typographic cards, and makes no request to TMDB.

Hiding, renaming and reordering

Playlists arrive with groups and names chosen by the provider, and those are frequently neither. Long-press or open the menu on any group or channel to hide it, rename it, or move it. Changes are yours and local, and survive a catalogue refresh.

Television and casting

Using Dawn Player on Android TV or Google TV

The same app runs on television, and appears on the Android TV home screen with its own banner. Navigate with the remote's direction pad; Back steps up one level and then out of the player.

Casting to a Chromecast

On Android, the cast button appears in the player when a Chromecast is on the same network. If it never appears: the device and the Chromecast must be on the same subnet — guest networks and some mesh setups separate them — and casting needs Google Play services, which some Android TV images ship without. Where casting is unavailable, the button is hidden rather than shown broken.

Privacy and data

What does the app send, and to whom?

Nothing to us — there is no account and no server of ours. Your credentials go only to your provider, to authenticate. The privacy policy lists every connection the app makes and what travels over it.

How do I delete everything?

Uninstalling removes all local data, credentials included. To remove one provider without losing the rest, delete that account in Settings → Accounts; its catalogue and cached artwork go with it.
